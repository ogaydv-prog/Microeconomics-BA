Why this is a two-sided market-power case

The same packers buy cattle from ranchers and sell boxed beef to wholesalers, restaurants and retailers. Concentration can therefore create monopsony power upstream and oligopoly power downstream. Concentration alone is not proof of collusion, but it can make coordination easier and reduce outside options on both sides.

Pandemic shock described in litigation

Directional index, pre-shock = 100; figures summarize claims and observed disruption reported in the Guardian, not a structural causal decomposition.

Consumer side

January 2020 to December 2025: average uncooked steak price rose from $7.653 to $12.512 per pound.

Meat margin

The key economic variable is the spread between the price paid for slaughter-ready cattle and the wholesale price received for boxed beef. A wider spread can reflect market power, supply shocks, capacity constraints, costs — or several factors together.

Identification problem

Parallel output cuts do not by themselves prove an agreement: firms facing the same cattle shortage and plant economics may react similarly. Antitrust analysis asks whether evidence shows coordination rather than common response.

Plaintiffs' and critics' theory

  • Temporary reductions in cattle buying and slaughter allegedly depressed cattle prices.
  • Capacity closures allegedly restricted boxed-beef supply and supported higher wholesale prices.
  • Similar production movements and communications are cited as possible evidence of coordination.
  • Ranchers, grocers, distributors and McDonald's have brought private claims; allegations remain contested.

Packers' and market-fundamentals explanation

  • The U.S. cattle herd is historically small after drought and herd liquidation, raising cattle input costs.
  • Plants below efficient utilization face high fixed cost per animal; closing a plant may be unilateral cost rationalization.
  • Common shocks can produce parallel conduct without an illegal agreement.
  • Tyson's beef segment reported an adjusted $143m Q1 FY2026 loss, illustrating that high retail prices do not imply every packer is profitable.
